Air compressor running even when the shop is idle: find the leaks

Level: Simple Industrial machinery · Air compressor · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

Compressed air is one of the most expensive forms of energy on a site. In poorly maintained installations, leaks commonly account for a large share of the air produced, sometimes 20 to 30%. Every leak makes the compressor run for nothing.

Warning signs

  • The compressor starts while nobody is using air.
  • Pressure no longer reaches the set point.
  • Hissing on the network in the evening.
  • Water in tools and pipework.
  • Shutdown on high temperature.

Likely causes and how to check

Likely causeHow to check
Leaks on the network (fittings, hoses, drains)Leak hunt in the evening, by ear and with soapy water
Blocked automatic drains, faulty dryerWeekly check of drains
Dirty cooler, room too hotCooler cleanliness, room ventilation
Low oil level, clogged air filterOil level, filter condition

What to do

  1. In the evening, with the shop idle: listen to the network, apply soapy water to fittings, and note how fast pressure drops.
  2. Repair the biggest leaks first.
  3. Check automatic drains every week.
  4. Clean the cooler, ventilate the room, replace the air filter.
  5. If pressure stays low after all this: have the compressor diagnosed.
Safety first. Never point compressed air at a person; depressurise before undoing a fitting. Your site’s rules and the manufacturer’s instructions always come first.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a compressed air leak cost?

A 3 mm hole on a 7 bar network releases roughly 10 litres of air per second. Producing that flow takes several kilowatts continuously: over a year, one leak of that size can cost thousands in electricity.

How do I find an air leak?

In the evening, when the shop is quiet: by ear, then with soapy water, which bubbles at the leak.
