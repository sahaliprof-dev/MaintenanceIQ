Pump making a gravel noise: how to recognise cavitation

Level: Intermediate Industrial machinery · Pump · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

A centrifugal pump that sounds like it is pumping gravel is usually cavitating: suction pressure is too low, small vapour bubbles form and then collapse on the impeller. The impeller wears silently for weeks, then the flow collapses. Borehole pumps, booster sets, process water, washing: every centrifugal pump can be affected.

Warning signs

  • A crackling noise, like gravel going through the pump.
  • Increasing, often irregular vibration.
  • Discharge flow or pressure falling or fluctuating.
  • The motor runs but the pump “pushes” less than before.

Likely causes and how to check

Likely causeHow to check
Blocked suction strainer or filterVisual inspection, machine stopped and locked out
Suction valve partly closedThe valve must be fully open
Level too low in the tank or wellWater level, dry-run protection
Liquid too hot, suction line too long or too narrowLiquid temperature, suction line layout
Air entering on the suction sideSeals, fittings and gland on the suction side

What to do

  1. Machine stopped and locked out: clean the strainer or filter, open the suction valve fully, tighten or replace the leaking seal.
  2. Throttle flow on the discharge side, never by closing the suction.
  3. Restore a sufficient water level before restarting, or protect the pump against dry running.
  4. If the noise persists, have the pump inspected: the impeller may already be eroded. Ordering the part before failure costs less than downtime.
  5. Record date, noise and pressure to follow the trend.
Safety first. Never work on a running pump: lockout/tagout before opening a strainer or filter. Your site’s rules and the manufacturer’s instructions always come first.

Frequently asked questions

Can I control pump flow by closing the suction valve?

No. Closing the suction lowers the inlet pressure and causes cavitation. Flow is throttled on the discharge valve.

Does cavitation really damage the pump?

Yes. Collapsing bubbles erode the impeller and make the pump vibrate, which also wears the bearings and the mechanical seal.
