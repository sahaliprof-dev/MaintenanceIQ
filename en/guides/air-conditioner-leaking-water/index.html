Air conditioner leaking water: where it comes from and how to stop it

Level: Simple Buildings and installations · Air conditioning · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

While cooling the air, an air conditioner also removes moisture from it: the condensate. It collects in a drain pan and leaves through a pipe, sometimes helped by a small condensate pump. If water drips from the indoor unit, this path is almost always blocked or interrupted.

Warning signs

  • Drops or a trickle of water under the indoor unit.
  • Damp marks on the wall.
  • Gurgling noise.
  • Condensate pump no longer starting, or alarm.

Likely causes and how to check

Likely causeHow to check
Condensate drain pipe blocked (deposits, algae, insects)Pipe outlet: does water flow out while the AC is running?
Condensate pump failedPump noise, float, power supply
Drain pipe with too little slope, or crushedPipe route, low points
Dirty filters: the evaporator ices up, then thaws all at onceFilter condition, presence of ice
Indoor unit not levelLevel of the unit on its bracket

What to do

  1. Switch off the air conditioner and isolate its power supply.
  2. Clean the filters.
  3. Clear the drain pipe as described in the manual (flush with clean water, no aggressive chemicals).
  4. Check that the condensate pump starts when its reservoir fills; if not, have it replaced.
  5. If water still leaks: have the slope and level checked by an installer.
Safety first. Isolate the power before any work: water and the indoor unit’s electrics do not mix. Your site’s rules and the manufacturer’s instructions always come first.

Frequently asked questions

Is it serious if the AC leaks a little?

Water damages walls, ceilings and equipment below, and can reach electrical parts. Deal with the cause quickly.

Why does the AC only leak in very humid weather?

The more humid the air, the more condensate the unit produces: a drain that is barely adequate then overflows.
