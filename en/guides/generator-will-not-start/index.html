Standby generator that won’t start: check the battery first

Level: Simple Industrial machinery · Generator set · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

Where the grid is fragile, the generator protects the whole business. Its main weakness: it almost never works, and a machine that does not run hides its faults until the day it is needed. The most common cause of a failed start is the battery.

Warning signs

  • The starter turns slowly or not at all.
  • The engine starts then stalls.
  • Persistent black or white smoke.
  • The transfer switch does not change over during an outage.

Likely causes and how to check

Likely causeHow to check
Flat battery or corroded terminalsResting voltage: about 12.6 V for a charged 12 V battery; below 12.4 V, recharge
Water and microbes in the diesel, clogged filtersDrain the water separator and tank bottom
Cooling: low level, dirty radiatorCoolant level and condition, radiator, hoses
Fouling from running at very light loadTest history: no load or real load?
Faulty transfer switchReal changeover test, not just engine start

What to do

  1. Check battery resting voltage monthly; keep terminals clean and tight and the charger on.
  2. Drain the water separator and tank bottom; keep the tank full to limit condensation.
  3. Run the monthly test under real load, aiming for at least a third of rated power for about thirty minutes, rather than off-load.
  4. Really test the transfer switch.
  5. Log every test: date, battery voltage, load, duration, observations.
Safety first. Diesel and exhaust gases: no heat source near a leak, test in a ventilated room, work with the engine stopped. Your site’s rules and the manufacturer’s instructions always come first.

Frequently asked questions

Should a generator run off-load every week?

A monthly test under real load is better: a diesel running for long periods at very light load fouls up (unburnt fuel, soot).

Why is there water in the diesel?

Condensation settles at the bottom of the tank, especially in hot, humid climates, and feeds microbes that clog filters. A full tank and regular draining limit the problem.
