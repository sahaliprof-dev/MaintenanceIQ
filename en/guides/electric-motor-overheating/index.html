Electric motor running hot: 5 causes to check

Level: Intermediate Industrial machinery · Electric motor · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

The three-phase induction motor is robust, but it mostly dies of three things: its bearings, heat and humidity. A motor running hotter than usual ages fast, because heat degrades the winding insulation. The good news: the cause is often simple.

Warning signs

  • Frame clearly hotter than usual at the same load.
  • Smell of hot varnish or plastic.
  • Thermal overload relay tripping.
  • Louder humming.

Likely causes and how to check

Likely causeHow to check
Dirty cooling fins or broken fanInspect the fan cover and fins, machine stopped
Mechanical overload (pump forcing, seized bearing)Current on all three phases compared with the nameplate; turn the shaft by hand, locked out
Room too hot or poorly ventilatedNote the ambient temperature with every reading
Low or unbalanced voltage between phasesMeasurement by a qualified electrician
Different currents between phases (loose terminal, worn contactor)Clamp meter on each phase, by a qualified person

What to do

  1. Clean the fins and the fan guard, machine locked out.
  2. Reduce the load or remove the cause on the driven machine.
  3. Ventilate the plant room.
  4. Have terminals tightened and cleaned, replace a worn contactor.
  5. Smell of hot varnish: stop and find the cause before restarting.
Safety first. Current and voltage measurements inside a live panel are reserved for qualified, authorised personnel. Your site’s rules and the manufacturer’s instructions always come first.

Frequently asked questions

What temperature is normal for a motor?

It depends on the motor. The most reliable approach is to compare with its own baseline, taken when it ran well, at comparable load and ambient temperature.

Why does phase imbalance overheat a motor?

A small voltage imbalance creates a current imbalance several times larger, which heats the winding.
