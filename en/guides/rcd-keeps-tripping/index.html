RCD keeps tripping: how to find the faulty circuit

Level: Intermediate Buildings and installations · Building electrics · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

A residual current device (RCD, called GFCI or ground-fault protection in some countries) trips when part of the current leaks to earth: a faulty appliance, moisture in a junction box or a damaged cable. It protects people. Never bypass it: look for the circuit, then the appliance, causing the leak.

Warning signs

  • The RCD trips at the same moment (rain, an appliance starting).
  • It will not reset.
  • Random power cuts.
  • Smell of heat or traces of moisture near a socket or box.

Likely causes and how to check

Likely causeHow to check
Faulty appliance (water heater, machine, motor, outdoor lighting)Unplug appliances, then plug them back in one at a time
Moisture in a box, an outdoor socket or a light fittingVisual inspection after rain
Damaged cable (rodents, building work, crushing)Insulation test by an electrician
Several small leaks adding up on the same RCDCircuit distribution, by an electrician

What to do

  1. Unplug all appliances and switch off the circuit breakers protected by this RCD.
  2. Reset the RCD, then switch the breakers back on one at a time: the one that trips the RCD points to the faulty circuit.
  3. On that circuit, plug appliances back in one at a time to find the culprit.
  4. If the fault is in the wiring or cannot be found: call a qualified electrician.
  5. Test the RCD regularly with its “test” button.
Safety first. Never bypass an RCD or replace it with a less sensitive one. Measurements and work inside distribution boards are for qualified, authorised persons only. Your site’s rules and the manufacturer’s instructions always come first.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the RCD trip when it rains?

Water gets into a socket, a box or an outdoor light and creates a leakage current to earth.

What is the test button for?

It simulates a leak to check that the RCD actually trips. Testing regularly, as the manufacturer recommends, makes sure it still protects you.
