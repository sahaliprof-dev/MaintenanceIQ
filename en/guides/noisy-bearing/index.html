Bearing whistling or rumbling: what to do before it fails

Level: Simple Industrial machinery · Bearings · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

Bearings are the wear part of every rotating machine: motors, pumps, fans, conveyors. They almost always warn before failing, through noise and temperature. A whistle is not as serious as a rumble or a knock.

Warning signs

  • A high-pitched, steady whistle.
  • A rumbling, rough noise.
  • Irregular knocking.
  • A bearing housing hotter than usual or than its neighbour.
  • Rising vibration on the housing.

Likely causes and how to check

Likely causeHow to check
Whistle: lack of greaseStethoscope before and after greasing
Heat right after greasing: too much greaseQuantity injected, purge of old grease
Rumble: damaged racewaysStethoscope, vibration trend
Knocking and strong heat: advanced damage, broken cageTemperature and noise compared with the other bearing
Repeated failures: misalignment, unbalance, contamination, poor fittingAlignment, seals, inspection of the removed bearing

What to do

  1. Whistle: grease with the grease and quantity specified in the manual, then listen again a few minutes later. If the noise returns within days, the bearing is already damaged.
  2. Rumble: order the bearing and replace it at the next planned stop.
  3. Knocking with strong heat: stop quickly, before the shaft and housing are damaged.
  4. After replacement, find out why it failed, or the new bearing will die the same way.
Safety first. Listen with a stethoscope on a fixed part only (bearing cap), never near moving parts. Your site’s rules and the manufacturer’s instructions always come first.

Frequently asked questions

Should I add more grease to silence a bearing?

No. Use the right grease and the right quantity. Too much grease makes a bearing run hot. Never mix incompatible greases.

How can I hear a single bearing?

With a mechanical stethoscope placed on the bearing cap, comparing with the other bearing and with an identical machine.
