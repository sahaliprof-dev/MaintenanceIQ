Reading a vibration spectrum: unbalance, misalignment, bearings

Level: Advanced Industrial machinery · Vibration analysis · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

A pocket vibration meter gives an overall level. A vibration analyser goes further: it splits vibration into frequencies (FFT spectrum), and each fault leaves a signature. The patterns below are the most widely used in maintenance; they guide the diagnosis, which should always be confirmed by a second method or an inspection.

Warning signs

  • The overall vibration level rises on an important machine.
  • The pocket meter is no longer enough to find the cause.
  • Faults return after every repair.
  • You are planning a shutdown and want to know what to replace.

Likely causes and how to check

Likely causeHow to check
Unbalance: dominant peak at 1× running speed, mainly radialCompare horizontal, vertical and axial; check rotor fouling
Misalignment: strong 1× and 2×, with high axial vibrationAxial readings on both sides of the coupling, alignment check
Mechanical looseness: many harmonics of running speedTightness of feet and housings, soft foot
Bearing defect: peaks at non-synchronous frequencies linked to bearing geometry, often with sidebandsDefect frequencies calculated from the bearing reference; trend and envelope
Belts: peaks at belt-pass frequency, below running speedBelt tension and pulley alignment

What to do

  1. Always measure at the same points (bearing housings) in all three directions, at normal load.
  2. Record the exact running speed: the whole spectrum is read in multiples of it.
  3. Compare with the machine’s baseline spectrum: the trend matters more than the value.
  4. Confirm the fault with a second method (temperature, noise, inspection) before dismantling.
  5. For critical equipment or complex faults, call a qualified vibration analyst.
Safety first. Measure only on fixed parts, away from moving parts, with guards in place. Your site’s rules and the manufacturer’s instructions always come first.

Frequently asked questions

Is a pocket vibration meter enough?

To trend a small machine, yes: it measures overall vibration velocity in mm/s. To find the cause of vibration, a spectrum is far more precise.

Why doesn’t 1× always mean unbalance?

Misalignment, a soft base or a bent shaft can also produce a 1× peak. Measurement direction and the other peaks help decide.
