Types of maintenance: corrective, preventive, condition-based, predictive

Level: Simple Maintenance method · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

There is no single right maintenance strategy, but a mix suited to each machine. Choosing the right type for each asset avoids both surprise breakdowns and replacing parts that are still healthy.

Corrective maintenance

You act after the failure. Acceptable for an unimportant machine, a duplicated one or one that is quick to repair. For a critical machine it is the most expensive: unplanned downtime, emergency parts, overtime.

Time-based preventive maintenance

You replace or inspect at fixed intervals, whether the part is worn or not: oil changes, filters, belts. Easy to organise, but failures still slip through between interventions and good parts get thrown away.

Condition-based maintenance

You monitor the real condition of the machine (temperature, vibration, noise, current, oil) and act only when the signs justify it. This is the MaintenanceIQ method: it uses the P-F interval, the time between a detectable defect and the failure.

Predictive maintenance

It goes further: from the trend of measurements it estimates when a defect will become critical, often with connected sensors and software. Useful for large plants; for a small site, well-run condition-based maintenance already delivers most of the benefit.

How to combine them

  • Critical machines (A): weekly condition monitoring + manufacturer’s preventive tasks.
  • Important machines (B): less frequent monitoring + preventive tasks.
  • Secondary machines (C): simple preventive, or even corrective.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between preventive and condition-based maintenance?

Time-based preventive acts at fixed intervals; condition-based acts when the measured condition of the machine justifies it.

Do I need sensors for condition-based maintenance?

No. Your senses, an infrared thermometer, a stethoscope and a pocket vibration meter are enough to start, as long as you measure regularly and consistently.
