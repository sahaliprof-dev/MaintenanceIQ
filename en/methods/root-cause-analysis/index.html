Root cause analysis: 5 Whys and the Ishikawa diagram

Level: Intermediate Maintenance method · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

Replacing a part without asking why it failed schedules the next breakdown. Root cause analysis works back from the visible effect to the cause which, once fixed, stops the failure from recurring.

The 5 Whys

Ask “why?” repeatedly until you reach a cause you can act on.

  1. Why did the pump stop? The bearing broke.
  2. Why? It overheated.
  3. Why? It lacked grease.
  4. Why? Greasing was not scheduled.
  5. Why? No greasing list existed for this machine.

Action: a greasing plan with point, grease, quantity and frequency. Replacing the bearing alone would have solved nothing.

The Ishikawa (fishbone) diagram

For problems with several causes, sort possible causes into families: Materials, Machine, Method, Manpower, Milieu (environment). In hot, humid or salty climates, the environment weighs heavily.

Good practice

  • Start from facts (removed part, photos, readings), not assumptions.
  • Keep the failed part: its appearance often tells the cause.
  • Check after the action that the failure does not return.

Frequently asked questions

Do I always have to ask exactly five whys?

No. Five is a guide: stop when you reach a cause that allows a concrete action.

When should I do a root cause analysis?

For any repeated failure, and any costly or dangerous one.
