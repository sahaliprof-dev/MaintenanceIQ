The P-F curve: your window to act before failure

Level: Simple Maintenance method · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

Almost all mechanical and electrical failures follow the same path: a defect appears, grows slowly, then faster and faster, until failure. The P-F curve describes this path and gives the rule for inspection frequency.

P and F

P (potential failure) is when the defect becomes detectable. F (functional failure) is the breakdown. The P-F interval ranges from a few days to several months depending on the defect.

Detect early, gain time

Typical order of appearance: vibration and ultrasound, particles in the oil, audible noise, heat to the touch, then smoke and breakage. A vibration meter detects a damaged bearing long before the ear, and the ear long before the hand.

The frequency rule

Inspect at least twice during the P-F interval, i.e. at an interval no longer than half of P-F. If a bearing defect takes about six weeks to go from “detectable” to “broken”, a reading every two to three weeks will catch it.

Frequently asked questions

Does the P-F curve predict the failure date?

No. It helps you enter the action window early enough to repair when you decide.

How do I know a machine’s P-F interval?

From experience, manufacturer feedback and failure history. Without data, start cautiously with frequent checks on critical machines.
