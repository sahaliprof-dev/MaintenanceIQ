Building a preventive maintenance plan in 6 steps

Level: Intermediate Maintenance method · by SAHALI, MaintenanceIQ

A preventive maintenance plan says what to do, on which machine, how often and by whom. It does not need software to exist: above all it must be simple, written down and followed.

The 6 steps

  1. Asset list: list every machine and photograph the nameplates.
  2. Criticality: rank machines A, B, C (10-minute grid).
  3. Manufacturer recommendations: oil changes, filters, lubrication, statutory inspections.
  4. Task lists: for each machine, clear actions (point, tool, expected value).
  5. Intervals: based on the manufacturer, criticality and the P-F interval; shorter in hot, humid or dusty conditions.
  6. Follow-up: record every job and every reading; review monthly.

Pitfalls to avoid

  • Trying to cover everything on day one.
  • A plan so heavy that nobody keeps it up.
  • Forgetting machines that rarely run: generator sets, standby pumps.
  • Never adjusting intervals with experience.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need a CMMS for a maintenance plan?

No. A spreadsheet or a logbook kept up to date is enough for a small site; a CMMS becomes useful as the number of machines grows.

How often should the plan be reviewed?

At least once a year, and after every major failure.
